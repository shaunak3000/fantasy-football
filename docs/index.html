The Rice Ball Draft Rule

Six more sophisticated draft strategies were benchmarked against the real 2025 draft. All six lost to a single sentence.

The entire strategy

Take the highest‑ranked player at a position you still need.

Executable from memory if every piece of software fails — which is the point. The tooling makes this rule fast and keeps you honest about roster limits; it does not replace it.

01 — The evidenceEverything cleverer lost

Each strategy replayed the real 2025 draft at all eight slots, fit only on prior seasons, then scored by what its players actually went on to do.

What the gap is actually made of

The winner (1793) and pure best‑available (1422) both order by raw consensus rank. The entire 371‑point difference between them is the roster‑cap filter — no model contributes anything. And best VOR at need losing to best available at need says it from the other side: converting rank into our own valuation destroys information, because consensus already prices the injuries and depth charts a rank‑to‑points curve cannot recover.

04 — The boardThe order, as the rule sees it

Top 120 of a 128-pick draft, ordered by expert consensus. This is the strategy — take the highest name here at a position you still need.

Nothing in this table is our own valuation. The rule reads straight down consensus rank, and the only thing that removes a name is a roster cap. That is deliberate: converting rank into our own points estimate cost 27 points in the benchmark, and ignoring roster caps entirely cost 371.

Two columns are context rather than instruction. ADP is where the wider ESPN population actually takes a player, and Δ is how far that sits from our rank — a large positive number means the room will let him slide, so taking him at our rank is a reach of that many picks. It cannot change a pick, because unlike everything else here it has never been backtested.
